- Why is this one different?
    - Alpöge has quite the track record by now, incl attaching his name to NS
    - Lean! (Verified complied by several outside sources and different implementations, which greatly reduce the likelihood of a bug)
    - The fact that we can be confident that this is indeed a correct proof after a couple of hours is itself a stark contrast to how, e.g., we had to wait for a couple of years until Andrew Wiles' proof of FLT was verified, incl the dramatic turn of an actual gap in his first approach
- Whole bag of mixed emotions: euphoria, confusion, curiosity, loss, excitement about the future

- There's been a long standing moaning about how fractured mathematics has become. There are no more universal genii, just specialised experts with a deep knowledge of one field who find it hard to communicate their ideas with colleagues, even when their area of expertise is adjacent.
    - Wiles' proof of FLT was celebrated in the public reception because it solved a centuries old question, but within mathematics much more because he solved it by proving the Taniyama-Shimura conjecture, now TSW or modularity theorem, thus building a bridge between the previously separate worlds of modular forms and elliptic curves.
    - Riemann's genius itself was translating the discrete world of prime numbers into the seemingly far away world of complex analysis via the zeta function, the generating function of the prime numbers, which allowed it tackle problems in number theory with an entirely different set of tools and methods.
- This is where AI truly shines: it's not just an expert in one field. It has read *all* the literature and is an expert in *every* field. This enabled it to come up with this unusual combination which humans were unlikely to come up with.
- Again, we don't know yet what kind of clever ideas and unusual combinations there are in the RH proof. The overwhelming consensus amongst human experts was that some entirely new techniques, maybe even new fields of mathematics would have to be developed before RH would fall. If everything checks out, maybe humans *did* in fact come up with all the required building blocks already, and even the smartest humans weren't able to keep all of them in their heads, so we were waiting for computers big enough to do that.
